# 3.3 Trend Analysis

We analyze temporal change in Yelp reviews from two complementary perspectives. First, VADER sentiment scores track whether review language becomes more positive or negative within selected business sectors. Second, annual PMI rankings track how the lexical context surrounding a commercially meaningful target term changes. Both analyses stream the prepared full corpus rather than loading all reviews into memory.

## 3.3.1 Sector Sentiment Trends

### Sector selection and method

We compare **Italian Restaurants** with **Fast Food**. These categories represent recognizable commercial sectors with sufficient review volume and contrasting service models. Sector definitions are configuration-driven rather than hard-coded. A review belongs to a sector when its Yelp category set contains every configured category:

- Italian Restaurants requires both `Italian` **and** `Restaurants`.
- Fast Food requires `Fast Food`.

This is exact category membership rather than substring or fuzzy matching. Yelp categories come from the business metadata's controlled category field, so fuzzy matching could introduce unrelated sectors and make the selection less reproducible. A review may belong to more than one configured sector; its text is scored once and then assigned to each matching sector.

VADER is applied to the original review text because punctuation, capitalization, degree modifiers, and negation contribute to its rule-based sentiment score. For review \(i\), the compound score \(x_i\in[-1,1]\) is classified as positive when \(x_i\geq0.05\), negative when \(x_i\leq-0.05\), and neutral otherwise. Scores are aggregated by review year and sector. The implementation maintains count, online mean, and online sample variance in each bucket, so memory depends on the number of sector-period buckets rather than the number of reviews.

For a bucket containing \(n\) reviews, the reported 95% confidence interval is the normal approximation

\[
\bar{x}\ \pm\ z_{0.975}\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}},
\]

clipped to the valid compound-score interval \([-1,1]\). These intervals express uncertainty in the annual mean under the sample-variance approximation; they do not correct for reviewer dependence, business clustering, or changes in the Yelp population.

The full run read all 6,990,280 reviews. It matched 660,089 distinct reviews to at least one selected sector and produced 672,366 sector-review assignments: 439,358 for Italian Restaurants and 233,008 for Fast Food. The difference arises because 12,277 reviews matched both sector definitions. Only 689 reviews lacked category metadata.

### Results

Across all matched reviews, the review-weighted mean compound score was 0.679 for Italian Restaurants and 0.366 for Fast Food; the corresponding mean star ratings were 3.781 and 3.029. The aggregate difference is descriptive rather than causal because the sectors differ in businesses, locations, customers, review volume, and temporal composition.

![Annual sector sentiment with 95% confidence intervals and annual review volume.](../../artifacts/sentiment/sentiment_annual_trend_report.png)

Italian Restaurants remained comparatively stable. Its annual mean compound score rose from 0.673 in sparse 2005 to a peak of 0.756 in 2008, then declined gradually to 0.652 in 2021 and 0.654 in partial-year 2022. The 2021 estimate is supported by 36,787 reviews with a 95% interval of [0.647, 0.658]. Positive-review share changed from 90.6% in 2010 to 85.0% in 2021, while negative share rose from 8.4% to 13.8%. The pattern indicates modest long-run weakening but continued strongly positive language.

Fast Food shows a much larger change. Its annual mean peaked at 0.626 in 2007, remained 0.614 in 2010, and then fell to 0.452 in 2015, 0.277 in 2019, and 0.158 in 2021. The 2021 estimate uses 24,474 reviews with a 95% interval of [0.149, 0.167]. Over 2010--2021, positive share fell from 84.7% to 55.7%, while negative share rose from 13.6% to 40.9%. The 2022 value increased slightly to 0.169, but 2022 is a partial year with only 1,227 Fast Food reviews and should not be interpreted as a confirmed reversal.

![Positive and negative annual review shares under VADER thresholds.](../../artifacts/sentiment/sentiment_class_shares_report.png)

Early years require similar caution. Fast Food has only ten matched reviews in 2005, producing a compound interval from -0.033 to 0.830; Italian Restaurants has 63. The confidence bands narrow sharply as review volume grows. Reporting review counts and uncertainty alongside the means prevents these sparse endpoints from appearing as precise sector estimates.

Annual text sentiment and mean star rating broadly move together, especially for Fast Food, providing a useful descriptive consistency check. They are not interchangeable: star ratings are explicit user labels, whereas VADER estimates polarity from text and can react differently to mixed statements, sarcasm, or domain-specific language.

![Annual text sentiment versus mean star rating; marker area represents annual review count.](../../artifacts/sentiment/sentiment_vs_stars_report.png)

The main business-facing result is therefore not merely that one sector has a lower average. Italian Restaurants shows a relatively stable positive baseline with gradual erosion, whereas Fast Food exhibits a sustained and substantially larger decline in both text sentiment and star ratings. This pattern can motivate investigation into operational factors, changing customer expectations, business composition, and location-specific effects, but the observational aggregate cannot identify which mechanism caused the change.

### Limitations

VADER is a general rule-based English sentiment model rather than a Yelp-specific supervised model. It does not reliably resolve sarcasm, implicit comparison, aspect-specific sentiment, or every domain expression. A review can praise food while criticizing service, but one compound score merges those aspects. Category membership is attached at business level and can overlap; the sector series therefore are not mutually exclusive experimental groups. Annual means also mix changes within businesses with entry and exit of businesses and reviewers. The normal confidence interval treats review scores as observations but does not model clustering by business or user. Finally, the dataset ends in January 2022, so the last year is incomplete.

## 3.3.2 Term Co-occurrence Drift

To examine whether the language used around a business aspect changed over time, we selected **service** as the target term. Service is commercially meaningful across Yelp business categories and sufficiently frequent for annual analysis: it occurred 2,318,574 times in 1,903,581 of the 6,990,280 reviews. We computed annual Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) rankings for 2005--2022 and retained the 20 strongest supported context terms in each year.

### Method

Reviews were streamed from the prepared JSONL file and tokenized with the project's custom tokenizer; the complete review collection was never loaded into memory. For every occurrence of an eligible lexical token, we generated directed centre-to-context events in a symmetric window of five raw token positions on each side. Stop words, conjunctions, and punctuation could not become centre or context terms, but their positions were retained. This prevents the filtering step from bringing originally distant words artificially close together. Windows never crossed review boundaries, and repeated occurrences contributed repeated events.

For target term \(t\), context term \(c\), and year \(y\), PMI was calculated from internally consistent directed pair-event marginals:

\[
\operatorname{PMI}_y(t,c)=
\log_2\frac{C_y(t,c)N_y}{C_y(t,*)C_y(*,c)},
\]

where \(C_y(t,c)\) is the number of `service`-to-\(c\) window events, \(C_y(t,*)\) is the number of eligible context events around `service`, \(C_y(*,c)\) is the context marginal for \(c\), and \(N_y\) is the number of all eligible directed pair events in that year. Each year was counted and ranked independently.

PMI can assign extreme scores to accidental rare pairs. We therefore required

\[
C_y(t,c) \geq \max\left(10,\left\lceil0.001\,C_y(t,*)\right\rceil\right).
\]

The relative component requires an observed conditional event rate \(P(c\mid t)\geq0.001\), while the absolute floor protects low-volume years. The effective threshold increased with annual data volume, from 10 events in 2005--2006 to 1,439 in 2018--2019. Candidates were sorted by decreasing PMI, decreasing co-occurrence, and alphabetical term as a deterministic final tie-break before retaining the annual top 20.

A sample-based sensitivity comparison supported a radius of five rather than seven. Across two sample halves, the mean Top-20 Jaccard overlap was 0.466 for radius five versus 0.421 for radius seven, and mean shared-term rank correlation was 0.749 versus 0.677. Radius five was therefore more stable while retaining a meaningful local context.

The main counting and ranking logic is:

```python
start = max(0, center_index - window_size)
end = min(len(terms), center_index + window_size + 1)

for context_index in range(start, end):
    if context_index != center_index and eligible[context_index]:
        context = terms[context_index]
        if center_term == target and context != target:
            target_cooccurrences[context] += 1

minimum = max(10, ceil(0.001 * target_pair_events))
pmi = log2(
    cooccurrence * total_pair_events
    / (target_pair_events * context_frequency)
)
```

### Results and temporal drift

The earliest year, 2005, contained only 854 reviews and four eligible terms, so 2006 is the first representative year in the compact table. PMI measures specificity to the target context rather than raw frequency.

| Year | Annual top five terms, in rank order (PMI) |
|---:|---|
| 2006 | impeccable (5.812), slow (5.335), customer (5.065), attentive (4.480), terrible (4.411) |
| 2010 | impeccable (5.111), customer (4.750), prompt (4.723), slow (4.491), poor (3.720) |
| 2015 | impeccable (4.527), customer (4.384), slow (4.077), prompt (3.995), poor (3.651) |
| 2020 | impeccable (4.441), customer (4.439), slow (3.942), prompt (3.621), poor (3.600) |
| 2022 | customer (4.423), impeccable (4.344), slow (3.945), prompt (3.811), poor (3.567) |

![Persistent annual PMI associations with service. Blank cells mean that a term was outside the annual top 20.](../../artifacts/pmi/pmi_service_persistent_heatmap_report.png)

The principal result is **stability with gradual reweighting**, rather than wholesale replacement of the service vocabulary. `excellent` appeared in the annual Top 20 in all 18 years, and eleven other persistent terms appeared in 16 or 17 years. From 2010 through 2021, the same five terms -- `impeccable`, `customer`, `slow`, `prompt`, and `poor` -- occupied the first five positions, with only a small ordering change between `slow` and `prompt` before 2013. This stable core mixes positive quality descriptors, customer-service framing, and negative experience descriptors. It describes the semantic field around service and is not an aggregate sentiment score.

Within this core, association strength changed. `impeccable` was ranked first from 2009 through 2021, but its PMI declined from 5.812 in 2006 to 4.344 in 2022. `customer` was rank 3 in 2006, rank 2 throughout 2010--2021, and rank 1 in 2022 with PMI 4.423. `prompt` remained highly ranked but declined from PMI 5.073 in 2007 to 3.811 in 2022. Negative operational terms remained persistent: `slow` held rank 3 from 2013 onward, while `poor`, `terrible`, and `horrible` repeatedly entered the Top 20. Positive speed terms such as `fast` and `quick` also persisted but generally occupied lower ranks, with median ranks 12 and 14. The main drift is therefore compression and reordering inside a durable vocabulary, not a simple transition from positive to negative service language.

Raw support shows why PMI must be read alongside frequency. In 2006, `impeccable` achieved PMI 5.812 from 12 co-occurrences; in 2020 it achieved PMI 4.441 from 936 co-occurrences. `customer` had 33,139 co-occurrences and PMI 4.439 in 2020. The adaptive support rule removes very weak pairs, but annual corpus size still affects uncertainty and comparability. The full result contains 344 ranked term-year rows: four in sparse 2005 and 20 in every subsequent year.

### Limitations

PMI identifies lexical association, not causation, topic prevalence, or service quality. Its value depends on the tokenizer, five-position window, eligibility rules, support threshold, and selected target. `Service` can denote customer service, table service, or a supplied facility, and the window method does not disambiguate these senses. The 2005 estimate is sparse, and 2022 is only a partial final year. Annual Top-20 membership is relative: a blank heatmap cell means that a term fell outside the displayed set, not that the association disappeared. Future work could repeat the controlled analysis for `price` and `atmosphere`, or within separate sectors.

## 3.3.3 Combined Interpretation

The two analyses answer different questions. VADER shows that Fast Food review polarity declined substantially while Italian Restaurant polarity remained comparatively stable. PMI shows that the vocabulary surrounding `service` was much more stable, with gradual changes in the strength and order of persistent positive and negative descriptors. These findings are compatible: aggregate sentiment can change even when the core service vocabulary remains recognizable, because the balance, frequency, and intensity of positive and negative contexts can change without replacing the associated terms. Neither analysis alone establishes a causal business mechanism, but together they distinguish polarity drift from lexical-association drift.
